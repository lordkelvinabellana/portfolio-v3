# Case Study — LexaDraft

---

# Project Name

## LexaDraft

Contract Template Builder + Workflow Automation Platform

---

# Problem

One of the founders at Ardexa owned a construction company and faced the same operational problem repeatedly:

contracts were being manually rewritten over and over again.

Most contracts followed nearly identical structures:

- company information
- owner details
- project location
- dates
- legal clauses
- signatures
- approval steps

Only specific values changed.

But every new contract still required manual editing, repeated review, and unnecessary administrative work.

This created:

- wasted operational time
- repeated manual errors
- slow contract turnaround
- inconsistent document quality
- unnecessary dependency on manual review
- delayed approval and sending workflows

The problem was not contract creation.

The problem was repetitive contract editing.

The business needed reusable structure, not repeated manual work.

---

# Why It Mattered

Operational friction inside businesses is expensive because it quietly steals time every day.

Manual contract work seems small until it happens hundreds of times.

For construction workflows especially, contract delays can directly affect:

- project timelines
- approval speed
- internal coordination
- legal review cycles
- client communication speed

The goal was not simply digitizing contracts.

It was reducing friction across the entire workflow.

Good operational software should remove repeated decisions.

That was the real value.

---

# My Role

## Product Builder + Frontend Lead

I worked directly with the founders to turn a real business pain point into a usable product workflow.

This included:

- frontend architecture
- product flow design
- template builder structure
- document generation workflows
- approval workflow planning
- export flow logic
- founder collaboration on real operational use cases

This was strongly product-driven work because the value depended on usability, not just technical correctness.

The system had to feel faster than manual editing or nobody would adopt it.

---

# Solution

Instead of manually rewriting contracts every time, LexaDraft transformed contracts into reusable templates.

The goal was:

build once

reuse many times

This removed repeated manual work while preserving flexibility.

The product became a workflow system, not just a document editor.

---

# Workflow Architecture

## Step 1 — Template Builder

Users create reusable templates where contracts are written once.

Instead of fixed text everywhere, repeated editable fields become variables such as:

- company name
- owner name
- dates
- project location
- payment terms
- signatures
- legal clause values

This created structured reusability instead of static documents.

Templates became assets instead of one-time files.

---

## Step 2 — Variable-Based Document Builder

After saving a template, users move to the document builder.

Instead of rewriting contracts, they simply:

- select a template
- fill variable values
- generate the final document

This dramatically reduces preparation time and prevents formatting inconsistency.

The workflow becomes:

input values

instead of

rewrite documents

That difference creates real operational speed.

---

## Step 3 — Export + Delivery

The system supports:

- PDF export
- Word export
- email sending

This ensured the workflow did not stop at creation.

The goal was full execution:

template → document → delivery

not just drafting.

This was important for real adoption.

Operational tools fail when they stop halfway.

---

## Step 4 — Future Approval + Signing Workflows

We also planned future workflow expansion including:

- legal team review
- approval routing
- signing workflows
- sharing systems before client delivery

This would allow the platform to move from:

document generation

to

full contract lifecycle management

This was the long-term product direction.

# Business Impact

The strongest value was reducing repeated manual work.

This created:

- faster contract preparation
- fewer repetitive editing mistakes
- cleaner document consistency
- faster internal review
- stronger workflow standardization
- less administrative dependency

Instead of treating contracts like isolated documents,

the platform treated them like repeatable operational workflows.

That created much stronger business value.

The product solved process inefficiency, not just document formatting.

---

# Challenges

The hardest part was designing flexibility without making the system too complicated.

If templates were too rigid, users would return to manual editing.

If templates were too flexible, the workflow would lose speed.

The system needed to feel:

structured enough to save time

but

flexible enough to stay useful

That balance was the real product challenge.

Another challenge was thinking beyond the visible UI.

The actual product value was not the editor.

It was the workflow behind the editor.

That required stronger operational thinking than normal frontend work.

---

# Key Lessons

LexaDraft reinforced that some of the best software products are not flashy.

They quietly remove expensive repetition.

The strongest products often solve boring operational pain points because those create the most consistent value.

It also strengthened how I think about internal business software:

the goal is not “better software”

it is

less unnecessary work

That principle applies to almost every product I care most about building.

If a system does not remove friction, it is probably not solving the right problem.
