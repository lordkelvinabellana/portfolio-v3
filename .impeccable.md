## Design Context

### Users
Primary: Mix of job recruiters, freelance clients, and potential collaborators — people evaluating capability quickly. Secondary: startup founders, tech leads looking for someone who can do both dev and product/automation work.

### Brand Personality
**Three words**: calm, precise, capable.

The portfolio should read like someone who has done real work and doesn't need to shout about it. Not flashy, not timid — just quietly confident. Like a well-maintained tool rather than a product landing page.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Dark (default). SaaS product-level dark — not "cool hacker," more like Linear or Craft: purposeful, refined.
- **Visual tone**: Editorial precision. Clean SaaS minimal. Condensed display headings with generous body whitespace.
- **References (feel)**: Linear.app, Raycast.com, Craft.do — dark, tight typography, restrained decoration
- **Anti-references**: Purple glowing portfolio clichés, blob backgrounds, icon+metric dashboard stats, gradient text, border-left accent stripes on cards
- **Photo**: Stays in hero. Clean concentric ring frame instead of decorative blobs.
- **Typing animation**: Kept but restyled — smaller, secondary text, not the visual centerpiece.

### Design Principles
1. **Capability over decoration** — every visual element either communicates something or gives breathing room. No decoration for decoration's sake.
2. **Condensed for impact, open for reading** — Barlow Condensed on large headings creates editorial pull; Geist keeps body text clean and scannable.
3. **Blue is honest** — primary accent is a clean confident blue (oklch ~258°), not AI purple or neon cyan. It reads as "professional developer," not "portfolio template."
4. **No cards within cards** — flatten the hierarchy. Experience timeline uses no card wrappers. Project cards stay contained. Certifications stay flat.
5. **Stats tell a story, not a dashboard** — numbers shown inline as text ("3+ years · 28 projects"), not as big metric blocks with icons.
